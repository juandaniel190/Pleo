# Pleo · Q2 2026 GTM Review — How to present this deck

A companion to `05_executive_deck.html`. The slides carry the data. This doc carries the **story** — what to say between the slides, what to pre-empt, what to skip past, and how the room should feel at each beat.

---

## The narrative in 3 sentences

> We're 48% through Q2 with €0 closed and a €56K gap. **That gap is a Mid-Market problem, not a sales effort problem** — and we can prove it with four quarters of data. We don't need a bigger push; we need one targeted intervention this quarter, and a re-baselining conversation with Finance for H2.

If the audience leaves remembering only those three sentences, the meeting succeeded.

---

## Before you start

**Read the room.** This is a tense meeting. €0 closed mid-quarter triggers defensive reactions — the CRO wants to look in control, the VP Sales wants to defend the team, Finance wants to understand if the H2 plan still stands. Your job is to take the conversation off "who failed" and put it on "where do we lever."

**Don't open with the miss.** Open with the operating decision. The miss is implicit in the title slide — you don't need to underline it.

**One sentence you'll hear, almost certainly:** *"So what changed this quarter?"* The honest answer is **nothing changed this quarter — the pattern has held for four quarters.** That answer is the whole reason slide 2 exists.

---

## Slide 1 · Where we stand

**What this slide does in the story:** establishes that the gap is *workable*, not catastrophic. Sets up the central question — *where* do we lever, not whether.

**How to open:** point at the bar, not the cards. "We're sitting at 82% of target on weighted pipeline. The €56K we're short is the entire conversation today." Pause. Let them look at the bar.

**The pivot on this slide:** the **upside card sits outside the bar deliberately**. Call this out. *"The €22K isn't in the bar because it isn't committed forecast — it's recoverable, not booked. The rest of the deck is about how to actually unlock it."* This framing buys you credibility: you're not double-counting, and you're already managing expectations downward on the soft number.

**Likely pushback:**
- *"Why are we using weighted pipeline, not gross?"* → Because gross would show €1.83M and make the gap look fictional. Weighted is what your CRO already commits to in the board meeting.
- *"€0 actuals at week 7 is alarming."* → Yes — and the next slide shows you it's the fourth quarter in a row this has happened. That reframes it from "Q2 problem" to "structural signal."

**What NOT to do:** don't dwell on the math of the €22K. If asked, the one-liner is "*15% re-engage × 22% historical win rate — both conservative floors.*" If they want to dig, the speaker note table at the end of this doc has the full breakdown.

**The AI marker (small purple sparkle) on the Upside card** — forward-pointer to slide 5. Don't introduce it yet; if someone notices, just say *"slide 5 — every AI marker has a queued automation."*

**Land the transition out loud:** *"The gap is real, but the more useful question is — why does this keep happening? Slide 2."*

---

## Slide 2 · The structural picture

**What this slide does in the story:** this is your **reframing slide**. It moves the room from "what went wrong this quarter" to "where the system is broken." Without slide 2 the whole deck reads as excuses; with it, the deck reads as diagnosis.

**How to open:** *"I want to widen the lens before we go narrower."* Point at the 4-quarter strip first, then the heatmap. Order matters — pattern first, location second.

**The pivot on this slide:** **the "one rep carried Q1" footnote is the most important sentence in the entire deck.** Without it, Q1's 24% looks like recovery and the structural argument collapses. Say it explicitly: *"If you take that one rep out, Q1 was also a 5% quarter. The trend is unbroken."* That's the moment the room shifts from execution-frame to system-frame.

**The thing nobody on this slide will say but you should:** Mid-Market and Enterprise are being run on the same playbook today. They shouldn't be. Mid-Market is **shorter cycles, smaller ACVs, higher volume** — and our coverage model treats them like Enterprise. That mismatch is what produces the 62% concentration.

**Likely pushback:**
- *"Are we sure it's structural and not just under-investment in Mid-Market?"* → Both can be true. Under-investment is the *cause*; the structural pattern is the *evidence*. Slide 4's action 3 is the test — if a focused 45-day sprint moves the needle, structural diagnosis is confirmed.
- *"Why isn't UK · Enterprise (Wise · €161K) the focus if it's the largest single deal?"* → It is — that's action 1 on slide 4. But one Enterprise deal doesn't change a quarterly pattern; a Mid-Market segment shift does. Both matter.

**What NOT to do:** don't get drawn into segment-by-segment debate at this slide. Park anything granular for slide 3. Your job here is to make one move: *quarterly pattern → segment concentration.*

**Land the transition out loud:** *"If Mid-Market is where the money lives, three operational frictions explain why those deals aren't closing. Slide 3."*

## Slide 3 · Three frictions

**What this slide does in the story:** this is the **owner-mapping slide**. The deck would feel preachy without it — "Mid-Market is broken" with no diagnosis is just a complaint. Slide 3 turns the complaint into an addressable list.

**How to open:** *"Three frictions. Each lives in a different part of the funnel. Each needs a different owner. That last part matters — if we put the wrong owner on the wrong friction, this 45-day plan fails."* That framing is what stops the inevitable *"the AEs need to call harder"* response.

**The pivot on this slide:** the **activity-zero overlay** is the strongest evidence in the deck. Say the line out loud: *"Six of sixteen reps logged zero activities in the last four weeks. Five of those six own at least one stale deal. The reps aren't lazy — they're sitting on pipeline with no urgency signal. Fix the signal, the activity follows."* That sentence is the whole "system, not coaching" argument compressed.

**The friction-by-owner mapping** (the table you should have in your head, not on the slide):

| Friction | Wrong owner | Right owner |
|---|---|---|
| Silence (34 stale deals) | Marketing | Sales Lead + RevOps alerting |
| Slippage (close dates pushing) | Individual AEs | VP Sales · forecast call discipline |
| Contracting bottleneck (3 deals stuck >90d) | Sales AE | Deal Desk + Legal |

Memorise this. It's the single most useful framing in the deck, and it doesn't appear on the slide explicitly — only as scattered "owner: ..." text.

**Likely pushback:**
- *"19% slippage — is that benchmarked against anything?"* → No formal benchmark in our data, but the trajectory is what matters. 0% → 19% in four months means reps have stopped trusting / committing to close dates. The forecast is moving week-to-week because of it.
- *"The Wise deal is one rep — shouldn't a manager just have called already?"* → That's literally action 1 on slide 4. The slide is essentially asking, *why hasn't this happened yet?* Don't be defensive — let the answer be the action.

**The AI markers on this slide** — all three frictions and the activity-zero callout carry a small purple **AI** chip. Each one is a forward-pointer to a specific automation on the slide-5 roadmap. Don't dwell on them here; the move is *"flag the problem, point forward, keep walking."* If someone asks, the cross-reference is: silence → re-engagement drafter, slippage → slip predictor, contracting → AI contract review, activity-zero → stale-deal predictor.

**What NOT to do:** don't read the funnel chart stage-by-stage out loud. It's there as evidence; the only stage you should name is **Contracting** (€110K, 3 deals, all stuck). The rest is supporting visual.

**Land the transition out loud:** *"Knowing where the frictions are makes the plan executable. Five actions, two horizons. Slide 4."*

## Slide 4 · The plan as OKRs

**What this slide does in the story:** this is **the slide that turns analysis into commitment.** Two columns, kanban-style — Q2 on the left, Q3 on the right. Each KR leads with the **measurable** (the number we will move); the initiative + owner sits beneath it. This format stops the meeting from drifting back into debate because every commitment has a metric attached to it.

**How to open:** *"Two columns. Left is Q2 — what we're doing in the next 45 days. Right is Q3 — what we're setting up structurally. Each KR is read top-down: the metric on top, the initiative + owner below. The room is approving these four objectives — 13 KRs in total, all measurable."*

**The pivot on this slide:** **read the metric, not the initiative.** Discipline yourself to point at each KR's top line — *"Recover ≥€22K · Slip rate 19% → ≤10% · Segment values 11 → 3 · Mid-Market attainment ≥50%"* — and let the audience read the initiative below at their own pace. The metric-on-top format is what makes the OKR feel committable; if you read the prose under each one you lose the rhythm.

**Q2 column — what to call out (don't read every KR):**

- **Obj 1 · KR1 (€22K recovery)** — *"Conservative floor — €654K stale × 15% × 22%. We expect to beat it; we won't commit to it."*
- **Obj 1 · KR2 (Wise)** — *"The single biggest, most defensible move on the slide. €161K, one phone call, this week."*
- **Obj 1 · KR3 (slip rate)** — *"The fastest-deteriorating trend in the dataset. 0% to 19% in four months. This is where forecast trust lives or dies."*
- **Obj 1 · KR4 (Finance)** — read the quote out loud: *"We're not going to hit Q2 — let's plan H2 on real numbers."* That single sentence is the entire Finance conversation.
- **Obj 2 · KR1 (picklist lock)** — *"Today the segment field has 11 spellings of 3 segments. Finance can't re-baseline against dirty data."*
- **Obj 2 · KR3 (activity-zero)** — *"Six of sixteen reps logged zero activities last month. This KR is the test of whether managers are managing."*

**Q3 column — Obj 3 is the structural play, Obj 4 is the system play:**

- **Obj 3 · KR1 (Mid-Market stale €836K → €500K)** — the sprint outcome scaled. The KR1/KR2/KR3 sequence tells the full Mid-Market story: clean up the book, prove the motion, then compress the cycle.
- **Obj 3 · KR2 (≥50% Q3 attainment)** — the structural test from slide 2 made measurable. *"Four straight quarters under 25%; we need to break the pattern."*
- **Obj 4 · KR1 (forecast accuracy ±15%)** — the proof that close-date discipline from Q2 carries forward.
- **Obj 4 · KR2 (1 AI pilot)** — **deliberately scoped down. We are not asking for four automations; we are asking for one pilot.** This is the line that prevents the AI roadmap from feeling like over-reach.
- **Obj 4 · KR3 (H2 sign-off)** — closes the loop opened by Q2 · Obj 1 · KR4.

**Likely pushback:**

- *"13 KRs is too many."* → Each Objective has 3 KRs (Obj 1 has 4 — the Wise call is too specific to bundle). They're sequenced; Q2 first. The team commits to Q2 today; Q3 we review at end of June.
- *"Why is Mid-Market attainment a Q3 KR, not Q2?"* → Because the sprint starts now but the *outcome* is measured at end of next quarter. We don't want to fake a Q2 win by stuffing pipeline.
- *"Can we change KR wording live?"* → Capture as follow-up. Don't litigate language. *"Happy to refine wording after — let's lock the substance now."*
- *"What's the AI marker?"* → Forward-pointer to slide 5. Each KR with an AI marker has a queued automation that could scale it.

**What NOT to do:** **do not** let "AI" become the focus on this slide. The OKRs are the focus; AI markers are signposts. If someone wants to drill into the automations, the answer is *"slide 5 — let's get there in 90 seconds."*

**Land the transition out loud:** *"That's the commitment side. Slide 5 names the impact, the AI roadmap that scales the OKRs, and the explicit ask of this room."*

## Slide 5 · The ask

**What this slide does in the story:** this is the **decision slide**, not the impact slide. The four impact cards are supporting detail. The AI roadmap is the future-state. The ask box on the right is the actual moment of decision.

**How to open:** read the title out loud — *"Decision asked of this room — approve the four OKRs and pilot one AI automation in Q3."* Then point at the AI roadmap and say: *"Every problem we flagged with an AI marker on slides 1 and 3 has a queued-up automation here. We're not asking for all four — we're asking for one pilot."*

**The pivot on this slide:** **the AI roadmap is sized as a one-pilot ask, not a four-tool ask.** This is critical. Spell it out: *"This is what RevOps becomes — but the commitment today is one pilot, one segment, in Q3. Proof first, scale second."* That framing keeps the slide from looking like a wishlist.

**The order to walk the visuals (left to right):**

1. **Impact cards (top-left)** — read out *"€22K capture, €1.34M protection."* Don't dwell. The protection number is the bigger story; let it land and move on.
2. **AI roadmap (bottom-left)** — point at each card briefly. *"Stale-deal predictor solves silence on slide 1. Re-engagement drafter scales the €22K upside. Slip predictor solves the slippage on slide 3. Contract review unblocks the 3 stuck deals."* Three sentences total — don't read each card.
3. **The ask (right)** — read the four OKR approvals out loud. End on the question.

**The sourcing recommendation (Partnerships ASP €93K vs Outbound €32K):** lives in the ask box as a single line, marked *"Not asked of this meeting."* Frame it: *"Flagged for the next planning cycle. Separate analysis."* Don't expand on it.

**Likely pushback:**

- *"€22K is small."* → Yes — and intentionally so. It's a floor we can beat, not a target we have to defend. The bigger number is the €1.34M protection.
- *"Four AI automations is too ambitious."* → That's why the KR is one pilot, not four. We learn what works on a single segment, then we scale what works. We're explicitly not asking for all four today.
- *"Why purple for AI? Is this a brand thing?"* → Yes — pink is our editorial accent (the gap, the headline), purple is the AI signal so the two don't blend. Same convention as the dashboard.
- *"What if the AI pilot fails?"* → Then we've learned a cheap, fast lesson. The pilot is scoped to one segment for one quarter; the downside is bounded. The four OKRs without the pilot still hold.

**The close:** don't end with "thank you." End with the explicit ask:

> *"What I need from this meeting is sign-off on the four OKRs and agreement on which automation we pilot in Q3. Can we walk out of this room with that?"*

Then stop talking. Let the silence work.

## What you don't want to do (anywhere in the deck)

1. **Don't apologise for €0 closed.** State it, frame it as a fourth-quarter pattern, move on.
2. **Don't read numbers off the slides.** The slides do that. You add context.
3. **Don't get pulled into individual deal debates.** If a rep's specific deal comes up, defer to "happy to look at that 1:1 after the meeting." Protect the systems-level argument.
4. **Don't let the meeting become a coaching review.** The activity-zero data is evidence of a system gap, not of bad reps. Keep saying *"system, not coaching."*
5. **Don't add a sixth action.** If someone proposes one in the room, log it and don't litigate it.

---

## Three answers to memorise (these will come up)

1. **"What changed this quarter?"** → *"Nothing changed this quarter. The pattern has held for four quarters. That's the reason we're framing this as structural."*

2. **"How confident are you in the €22K?"** → *"High — but only because we picked conservative inputs. 15% re-engage is an industry floor; 22% is our blended historical win rate. We expect to beat it; we won't commit to it."*

3. **"What if the Mid-Market sprint doesn't work?"** → *"Then we've learned the diagnosis was wrong in 45 days instead of 90. The actions are reversible. The actions on either end — Wise and Finance re-baseline — deliver value regardless."*

---

## The one thing to remember walking in

**You are not defending the quarter. You are reframing it.** Every minute spent debating Q2 specifics is a minute not spent securing the five actions. The success criterion is *did the room sign off* — not *did the room agree the analysis is complete.*
